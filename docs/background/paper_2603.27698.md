# arXiv:2603.27698 — "Ink Detection from Surface Topography of the Herculaneum Papyri" (Angelotti, Nicolardi, Henderson, Seales)
Read 2026-09-16 (HTML version). What it prescribes and what it does not:
- Data: 3-D optical profilometry (Sensofar S lynx 2, confocal) of mechanically opened PHerc. 248, 250, 500P2: 16 letters in 14 samples; 0.34 µm lateral sampling, 8 nm vertical sensitivity. Labels drawn on co-registered brightfield photos.
- Method: 2-D nnU-Net (default hyperparameters, Dice+CE) on heightmaps normalised to [0,1] → uint16; no plane subtraction, no tilt correction; NaN pixels inpainted (Telea). No classical topographic filter is proposed; they state explicitly that "filtering to isolate roughness alone does not reliably separate inked letters from the substrate".
- Results: held-out Dice 0.890 [0.864, 0.905] at 0.34 µm; 0.754 at 1.02 µm; 0.586 at 1.36 µm; 0.516 at 2.04 µm; 0.478 at 2.72 µm; 0.467 at 10.88 µm (matched-resolution training). Pixel sizes ≥ ~2 µm mutually indistinguishable. z-binning to Δz = Δx changes median Dice by ≤ 0.026 (vertical quantisation is not the bottleneck). Leave-one-papyrus-out at 0.34 µm: mean Dice 0.691. Conclusion: "reliable segmentation benefits from lateral sampling on the order of 1 µm or finer".
- Consequence for this route: the paper does NOT give a CT method to copy; section 5 of the brief is the specification. Its resolution curve is a prior: at 2.4 µm lateral sampling with perfect height measurement, a learned topographic detector reaches Dice ≈ 0.5 on single-letter crops (their Dice at coarse sampling is close to what a blob-shaped prior would give on letter-centred crops, so it is weak evidence of residual signal); at 9–11 µm ≈ 0.47. CT height maps are far noisier than profilometry (partial volume, reconstruction). Expectation for GATE-T1: the pure relief term r is likely weak; the face-intensity residual terms (e, a) carry the rest. GATE-T2 at 9 µm is expected to fail on topography alone.
- The brief's Challenge-tutorial description of crackle ("cracked mud raised slightly above the papyrus") is the phenomenology we test; the paper adds that inked regions appear smoother in SEM (ref. 13), i.e. a possible NEGATIVE roughness residual, so the relief term's sign is not assumed: z(r) is used signed as specified, and the sign of the text-vs-blank difference is recorded.
