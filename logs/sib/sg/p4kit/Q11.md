## What the terms say (verbatim)

1. **The Title Prize section itself (S1)** contains **no open-source clause and no data or weights clause**. Its requirements are the mesh, the image, programmatic generation, the scan, the scale, traceability, the methodology, false-positive mitigation, held-out validation and review, plus:
   > "As with the Grand Prize, you must not make your discovery public until the prize is officially announced. We will work with you to announce your findings."
   The First Letters clause "open sources their code, data, model weights and results (after winning the prize)" belongs to the First Letters section only.
2. **General Terms and Conditions** (S1, page bottom, which the page says "also apply", and identical in the Title Prize form, S2):
   > "You agree to make your method open source if you win a prize. It does not have to be open source at the time of submission, but you have to make it open source under a permissive license to accept the prize. [...] Prize winner must provide payment information to Scroll Prize, Inc. within 30 days of prize announcement to receive prize."
3. **The organizers' data policy for datasets** (S1, 2027 Grand Prize, "Compute and data conditions"). It does not bind the Title Prize, but it states their intent:
   > "Any dataset created or used to train ML models must be published publicly, under a CC-BY-NC 4.0 license."
   > "If pseudo-labeling (or iterative labeling) is used to improve and train ink detection models, the datasets and checkpoints at every stage must be released under a CC-BY-NC 4.0 license."
   The same section says: "You may use any information or resource that is publicly available (...) provided that use is permitted by that resource's own license or terms."
4. **Site footer (S1):** "Content licensed CC BY-NC 4.0 unless otherwise specified". **Data (S3):** CC BY-NC 4.0.

## What we must, may and must not publish (Title Prize)

**MUST, to accept the prize:**
- Publish the **method**, meaning the project's own code (tiling, display, harness, scoring, mesh crop and flatten, render, the kit), under a **permissive licence** (for example MIT), publicly, after winning.
- The released code must not embed organizer data or EduceLab-licensed meshes.
- The payment information and the terms acceptance are OWNER-ONLY.

**MAY:**
- After the official announcement, publish the derived images, maps and numbers under **CC BY-NC 4.0** with attribution. They derive from CC BY-NC data (S3), so they are never relicensed permissively.
- Publish the project's own non-derived results (counts, statistics) with the code.

**MUST NOT:**
- **Publish anything about the discovery before the official announcement** (embargo). The project's branches must not be made public before then, and no public post or message may be made.
- Relicense organizer CT, segments or predictions permissively.
- **Redistribute the 5 EduceLab-licensed meshes at all**: not in git, not in a package, not publicly (S4). A submission package must therefore use the official S3 (CC BY-NC) mesh of the candidate segment, never an EduceLab mesh.

**Weights:** the project trained no model for Paris 4, so there are no weights to publish.
- M1 is official output.
- P4-L is model-free.
- P4-O uses third-party public checkpoints, governed by their own licences. We release only our inference code.

If a project-trained checkpoint ever enters a Paris 4 lineage, release it and its data under CC BY-NC 4.0, consistent with the organizers' dataset and checkpoint rule, and confirm with the organizers.
