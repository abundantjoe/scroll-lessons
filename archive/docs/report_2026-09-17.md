# Papyrus-subtraction route: calibration report (2026-09-17, in progress)

## Question
Can ink on carbonised Herculaneum papyri be recovered from CT surface volumes by physically motivated, pre-declared measures
(sheet topography, face-brightness residuals), validated against (a) an independent ink model and (b) physically verified text?

## Data
- w028 (PHerc. 1667), 2.399 µm surface volume, 7 crops of 4 cm²; reference = team ink model (not ground truth).
- Scroll 1 (PHerc.Paris.4), 2.4 µm surface volumes of Grand-Prize segments 20231005123336 (4 crops of 1.5 cm) and 20231210121321
  (4 crops), reference = the GP hand labels of papyrologically read text, registered globally (scale + offset by correlation) and per crop
  (translation), with the team model's 0.94–0.98 on the same crops as the alignment sanity check.

## What the data say (measured, not assumed)
1. Sheet topography carries no ink information at 2.4 µm CT (thickness AUC 0.48–0.50 on both scrolls), as the profilometry paper's
   resolution curve predicts; "crackle" relief is fibre-scale noise here.
2. The only pixel-wise physical contrast is a weak brightness excess of the bright band, peaking at the top-face crossing and 5–10 µm
   inside the bottom face; per-depth AUC never exceeds 0.58 on verified text (D2). Its 22-number depth template transfers from
   PHerc. 1667 to Scroll 1 unchanged.
3. Linear methods over this contrast saturate at 0.65–0.68 px held-out; more features fit the training crop better and generalise no better.
4. The team model reads the same crops at 0.86–0.98: the information is in the CT, and it is spatial (stroke and letter shape), not a
   per-pixel physical quantity. Any honest "physics-only" claim of readable text at this resolution is therefore unsupported by these data.
